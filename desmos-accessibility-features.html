Making math accessible

Crafting new experiences in the Desmos calculators
to support blind and low-vision students.

Two software engineers and I spent years learning from blind and low-vision students, Teachers of the Visually Impaired (TVIs), and accessibility experts to improve the accessibility of the Desmos calculators. Each project was a new design challenge that required close partnership with our users, often in the absence of existing accessible design patterns. Every piece of feedback we received and person who shared their perspective with us was such a gift. One of my favorite parts of improving accessibility is getting to learn from such an incredible community.

Braille Mode

How might they best support students using a refreshable Braille display alongside a computer? How would the TVI know where a student made a mistake and invite them to try again? How would the TVI do this if they rarely used Nemeth, a Braille code specific to math? With this in mind, we added typeset math alongside the Braille so someone working with a person using a refreshable Braille display could see the math on the screen.

Bonus: I designed a Braille font for this project that we titled "Abraham" after Abraham Nemeth, the creator of Nemeth Braille.

Audio Trace

This was an incredibly fun design challenge. How can you help a student understand a graph without being able to see it? We ultimately developed a mode called "audio trace" that allows a user to hear an audio representation of a graph, including different audio cues for intercepts and negative or positive values.

Tactile Graphics

Embossed graphics are another way for blind or low-vision students to interact with a graph. We created a way to format and export a graph so TVIs or their students could seamlessly create tactile graphics on the most common embossers.
